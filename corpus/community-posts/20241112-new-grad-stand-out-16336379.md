Three ways to effectively stand out as a new grad

I’ve been getting a flood of messages and connection requests on LinkedIn asking for jobs. It’s reached a point where I just can’t keep up with replying.

I feel bad because I know some of these people put in real effort — they tried to send a thoughtful (but still generic) message. I can understand the situation on their end. But since I can’t respond to each person individually, I’m writing this post to share some advice. If you’ve messaged me or plan to, please take a moment to read this.

The job market this year is incredibly tough. I even heard that one position at Netflix received over 78,000 applications. It’s wild. How can anyone stand out, especially new grads with little to no work experience?

The problem is that the “traditional job application” process has been overused and is now broken. Things like grinding through leetcode problems, applying for hundreds of roles, and using LinkedIn to find every job listing just aren’t working effectively for employers or job seekers, because the precision and recall are ridiculously low.

In this environment, quality matters way more than quantity. Here are three key steps to help you stand out:

1. Build an impressive project. Skip the typical class or Kaggle projects — they don’t add value and might even make a negative impression. Instead, check out projects from the latest Y Combinator batch and try to replicate one using AI tools. Just a thought.

2. Show consistent effort. If you’re applying for tech roles, keep a well-maintained GitHub. For non-tech roles, consider creating a blog or YouTube channel. Publishing content regularly (ideally every day) shows commitment and builds credibility.

Most candidates put up their GitHub but has only a few commits. You can easily stand out.

3. Do deep research on a few companies. Don’t send generic outreach messages. Instead, focus on understanding a handful of companies really well, and tailor your messages specifically to them.

These steps take time and effort, but they can make a real difference in setting you apart.
